# A Book on Mom: Memories, Archives, and Legacy

## Introduction

When you grow up in a house with six children, life is rarely defined by quiet, isolated moments. It is a fast-moving, high-capacity machine. In that kind of environment, a parent who is always there—the stay-at-home mother managing the daily rhythm—can sometimes feel less like a distinct "event" in your life and more like the very environment you swim in. 

My dad was an emergency room physician whose presence, while deeply loving, often came in rarer, more intense episodes. My mom, on the other hand, was the constant. She was the steady, reliable background of my entire childhood. Because of that, capturing her story requires looking past the grand headlines and examining the daily machinery she built, the quiet strength she carried, and the environment of practical love she maintained so that our family could simply *exist* together.

This book is dedicated to her—not just as the backbone of our massive family, but as an individual with her own passions, her own sharp administrative mind, her own resilience, and a life that touched dozens of children, scouts, and relatives.

---

## Chapter 1: The Foundation and Passions

Before she was the project manager of a chaotic household, and long before she was surrounded by a mountain of school projects and bulk groceries, she was her own person with distinct tastes that have remained entirely her own over the decades. 

Her enduring love for the *Star Trek* franchise is a thread that runs deep. From reading the paperback books in the early days of the series to watching *Voyager* and following Captain Janeway to pass the time and find comfort during quiet moments, science fiction has always been her space to escape. Paired with her love for the classic sound of The Eagles—firmly rooting her musical taste in the classic '70s era—these preferences are uniquely hers. 

---

## Chapter 2: The Family Archivist

She has always possessed a deeply analytical, highly organized mind, which manifested most clearly in how she preserved our family history. 

Long before digital cloud storage and modern photo apps, family vacations were documented through the lens of film cameras, physical film development, and meticulously bound photo albums. But she didn't just drop photos into sleeves. She would map out our trips with traced route maps, tucking extra travel documents, brochures, and notes into the pages to create a complete historical record of where we had been. 

Beyond travel, she maintained detailed, individual crates and folders of art, schoolwork, and milestones for all six of us kids. Years later, once we had all finally moved out of the house, she made sure each of us took our own crate of childhood history with us—guaranteeing that none of those memories were ever lost to time.

---

## Chapter 3: The Craft Closet & Resourcefulness

In a house with six kids, resourcefulness isn't a hobby; it's a survival tactic. My mom mastered this through her legendary craft supply closet and her basement reserves. 

There was one specific closet in the house dedicated entirely to bins of arts, crafts, and project materials, perfectly sorted and always ready for action. Knowing that school projects, dioramas, and art assignments were an endless, rolling cycle, she constantly saved heavy-duty cardboard boxes and thick cardboard tubes down in the basement. 

The true beauty of this system was its longevity. Because my youngest brother, Luke, is only a year older than the very first grandchild, that craft stash never actually retired. It smoothly transitioned from serving her own children to serving the grandchildren and supporting local Scouting activities. When a Scout project or a last-minute school assignment popped up decades later, the supplies were still right there, waiting in the basement.

## Chapter 4: Running the Machine

Feeding and managing a family of eight requires the logistics of a small military operation. 

In her later years, her domain became the kitchen command center: stationed at the stove, watching a show on the computer, smoking a cigarette while the exhaust fan hummed above her—a completely honest, grounded portrait of her taking a well-earned moment of peace in her own space. 

Back when the house was full, grocery shopping was a major logistical expedition. It wasn't a quick errand; it was an event requiring two shopping carts heaped completely full of goods. The absolute baseline of household survival meant buying 14 gallons of milk every single week just to keep up with the demand. 

To feed the crowd, she mastered hearty, practical, stretchable meals. The rotation was built on staples: spaghetti with meat and sauce, hamburger or venison with onions, meatloaf, and scalloped potatoes purposefully engineered to give new life to leftover holiday ham. 

---

## Chapter 5: The Integrated Family System

No single person could run that household alone without burning out, so my parents built an integrated team system. We kids were woven directly into the daily operations. 

If you weren't helping cook dinner, your job was setting the table, putting food away, or washing the dishes. One of the classic daily roles was the dinner bell relay—someone's specific job was to run through the house and round up all the siblings to let them know food was ready. 

When it came to side dishes, she instituted a brilliant, low-stress system: the pantry run. In the kitchen pantry, she kept an active row of canned corn, beans, and peas. When dinner rolled around, we kids were given the task to "pick out two cans" to decide the evening's vegetable. 

And for community gatherings and potlucks, her signature dish was the quintessential family staple: the Beef and Bean Bake, also known as cowboy beans. A massive batch of brown beans and hamburger, elevated by her specific touches of molasses, mustard, and a secret seasoning blend that made it an instant crowd-pleaser.

## Chapter 6: The House That Grew With Us

The physical layout of my parents' house tells the story of our family's growth. The original structure—what we always called the "old side"—contained two downstairs bedrooms, a living room, and the original kitchen. 

When the family expanded, my parents built a massive addition that completely shifted the home's center of gravity. The old living room became the new kitchen, the front bedroom became the dining room, and the back bedroom became the home office. 

Instead of tearing out the old kitchen, they brilliantly repurposed it into the laundry and overflow room. The original refrigerator stayed right in place, acting as the primary staging ground for our 14 gallons of milk. The old food pantry next to it was converted into a utility storage hub for tape, glue, the electric griddle, and random household essentials, keeping the new kitchen clean and uncluttered. 

Meanwhile, the true deep-reserve bulk inventory—all our extra canned goods—was housed downstairs in the basement room we always called the "fruit room." When the active upstairs pantry ran low, you simply made a supply run down to the fruit room to restock.

## Chapter 7: The Woman Behind the Family

While her role as a mother defined so much of her daily life, her professional and personal identity was anchored by her training as a nurse. 

That medical background fundamentally shaped how she approached raising a family. When one of us woke up sick, there was no dramatic panic or fussing. My dad was an emergency room physician and she was a nurse, so sick days meant an efficient trip to the well-stocked home medicine cabinet, clear instructions to rest, and absolute confidence in the care we received. 

Her sharp, analytical mind also extended into modern logistics. She has always been the administrative anchor of family records, adapting seamlessly to software tools and digital record-keeping to keep our shared family history straight. 

---

## Chapter 8: The Scouting Partner & Community Leader

Her organizational talents and love for the outdoors didn't stop at the front door. She threw herself entirely into community leadership through the Scouting program. 

She was deeply involved as a leader in Cub Scouts and Girl Scouts for all of us, eventually serving officially as an Assistant Scoutmaster for Troop 6017. She wasn't a parent who just dropped kids off at the edge of the woods; she was right there on campouts, guiding youth, managing first aid, and keeping the troops organized. 

One of the most tangible memories of this era sits on a shelf today: a thick chunk of a log, cut during a pirate-themed Cub Scout day event using a heavy two-person crosscut saw. While we pulled the blade together, she was right on the other end. Once the chunk was cut, we heated up a branding iron and stamped a skull-and-bones design directly into the wood—a permanent physical artifact of teamwork that outlasted the campfire.

## Chapter 9: The Unshakable Strength

Raising six children while managing massive family logistics is a formidable challenge, but carrying it all while facing severe health crises reveals a level of resilience that is almost impossible to overstate. 

Years ago, she was diagnosed with brain aneurysms—including one positioned in a critically dangerous zone near her eyes. She underwent a major, high-stakes surgical procedure where doctors harvested an artery from her thigh to build a bypass. 

What followed was a terrifying close call in recovery. Due to a careless medical error when a nurse sedated her simply because she kept asking to have her hair washed, her blood pressure dropped dangerously low. That drop caused the surgical graft to clot off and seal entirely. In that critical moment, it was human biology and sheer willpower that kept her alive. Her survival relied entirely on the remarkable collateral circulation of the circle of Willis—a biological lifeline that compensated for the blocked artery. 

Decades later, she would benefit from pioneering, early experimental stenting to manage her remaining aneurysms. Through every hurdle, every Scouting campout, and every day of raising a massive family, she carried an unshakable strength that proved her toughness was woven into the very fabric of her life.
