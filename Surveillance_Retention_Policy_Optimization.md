# Project: Security Surveillance Retention & Storage Lifecycle Analysis

## Executive Summary
Led a capacity planning and policy development project to resolve a critical security coverage gap. Engineered a vendor-agnostic, dynamic storage calculation model and retention policy to ensure compliance with NIST SP 800-171 and NIST SP 800-53 physical security requirements.

## Problem Statement
The organization lacked adequate archival retention, resulting in the loss of critical evidence during a security incident due to premature data overwriting. The objective was to define a sustainable, hardware-agnostic storage architecture that met defined forensic recovery requirements while accommodating a heterogeneous mix of camera systems and resolutions.

## Methodology (Risk-Based Analysis)
*   **Incident Impact Assessment:** Evaluated the operational impact of existing data retention gaps following an identified security incident.
*   **Dynamic Capacity Planning:** Developed a technical storage calculation formula capable of adapting to diverse environments. The model accounted for:
    *   **Hardware Variables:** Mixed camera densities, varying megapixels, and differing resolutions.
    *   **Compression Metrics:** Fluctuating bitrates and codec efficiency across multiple standards (e.g., H.264/H.265).
    *   **Variable Retention Forecasting:** Designed the mathematical model to allow stakeholders to input desired retention targets (e.g., 30, 60, or 90 days) and instantly output the required terabytes (TB) for budget and procurement forecasting.
*   **Framework-Aligned Policy Development:** Drafted a formal technical standard for surveillance retention aligning with NIST SP 800-171 and NIST SP 800-53, establishing strict protocols for periodic audits and hardware lifecycle management.

## Technical Skills Demonstrated
*   **Forensic Readiness:** Designed infrastructure models specifically to ensure the availability and integrity of physical security evidence.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Proactively identified and remediated a critical physical security risk before future incidents could occur.
*   **Technical Documentation:** Transformed complex data storage variables into an actionable, dynamic forecasting tool and policy for non-technical stakeholders.

## Key Takeaways
*   **GRC Fundamentals:** Demonstrated the ability to map physical security needs to stringent federal compliance frameworks.
*   **Strategic Planning:** Successfully balanced the technical requirements of high-resolution surveillance with the budgetary constraints of storage procurement by empowering leadership with flexible, data-driven planning tools.
